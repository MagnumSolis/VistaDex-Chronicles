# Executive Summary

**VistaDex** is a screening tool that checks a startup or research idea's specific factual claims — "nobody else is building this," "the market is worth $X," "a big company could not easily copy this" — against real, live evidence, and separately checks the idea against a purpose-built database of startups that already tried something similar and failed, pivoted, or quietly shut down.

It exists because of a real, repeated failure the team experienced directly: while researching startup ideas with AI assistance, confident claims of "this is novel and unclaimed" were made and believed multiple times — and multiple times turned out to be wrong on direct verification. Real, sometimes well-funded, competitors already existed. That is the motivating problem, and it is one that happens to founders, students, and accelerator applicants constantly.

**The honest market finding:** a generic version of this — "AI checks your startup idea" — already exists and is crowded (Preuve AI, IdeaProof, ValidatorAI, DimeADozen, and others, some dating to 2022). Building another one of those loses. What survives scrutiny is a narrower, more defensible version: sold to **institutions screening many ideas at once** (accelerators, universities, VC funds), differentiated by a **proprietary database of dead and pivoted startups**, and built with an explicit **accuracy methodology** so its own verdicts can be trusted — the same discipline the team's parallel HCI research is built around, applied to a second, independent product.

## The verdict, in short

- **Kill:** a generic, founder-facing, self-serve "check my idea" app. Crowded, three-plus years old, already surfaced directly by Google's own AI answers.
- **Keep, narrower:** an institutional screening tool built around a proprietary graveyard database, with an honest, documented accuracy methodology as part of the product — not an afterthought.

## One-line USP

> "Every other tool tells you if your idea looks good today. We tell you if it already died once — and we're honest about how sure we actually are."
