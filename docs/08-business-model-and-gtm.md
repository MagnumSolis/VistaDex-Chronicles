# Business Model & Go-to-Market

## Pricing

Priced **per cohort or per institution** — a program pays to screen a batch of applicants, a fund pays to screen a pipeline of deals — not per individual founder. A limited free or low-cost self-serve tier can exist purely as a discovery funnel, but the actual business is the institutional contract.

This avoids competing on price against the $9–$49 apps already serving individual founders, and instead sells into a budget line that already exists for screening and diligence tools.

## Stakeholders

| Segment | Why they buy | Realistic first move |
|---|---|---|
| **Accelerators & university innovation programs** | Screen large batches of applications; documented frustration with AI-written, look-alike submissions | Pilot with a program already accessible — including the AICTE/YUKTI-affiliated program already being engaged with |
| **VC funds & angels** | Already pay for diligence tooling; need faster first-pass competitive checks | Approach after the accelerator pilot produces a track record |
| **Corporate innovation / venture teams** | Scouting for acquisition targets or internal R&D gaps | Longer sales cycle — treat as a later-stage segment |
| **Individual founders** | Same pain personally experienced by the team | Free/low-cost tier only, as a funnel — not the core business |

## Sequencing

Build the Verifier and a first version of the graveyard database → pilot with one real, reachable accelerator cohort (PIEDS) → use that real usage data to make the graveyard database genuinely useful → approach VC funds once there's a track record to point to.

## Risks & honest limitations

- The founder-facing version of this idea is already a crowded, three-year-old market — this only works as the narrower, institution-facing version described here.
- The graveyard database only becomes valuable once it has real coverage — a database of fifty entries will not impress anyone; this is a multi-month build, not a weekend project.
- Major AI platforms could plausibly ship a similar capability using data access they already have; the graveyard database and the institutional sales channel are the only durable defenses against that, not the underlying AI technique itself.
- Publishing a claim that a real company or founder "failed because of X" carries reputational and potential legal risk if wrong — the human-review step before publishing any entry is not optional polish, it's a requirement.
- This idea has been checked for competitors and structural risk, but not yet tested with a single real customer — that's the next real step, not a finished conclusion.
